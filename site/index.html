Your team's game, in a corner.

A small window that stays on top and follows MLB games as a few quiet pieces. Free and open source.

Download Source code

As big as you can spare.

Drag the corner and it shows more, or less.

Dot
A pill with the score, the inning, the bases and the outs.
Bar
Adds the count and the latest pitch or play.
Field
Team-coloured pieces with runners' names, the flight of the ball, every pitch numbered in the zone.
Full
Adds the matchup and the line score.

Made to be glanced at.

  • Follows your team. Live when they play, otherwise a countdown with the probable starters.
  • Hides in a keystroke. Ctrl+Alt+Shift+B hides every window, and low-key mode turns it into a plain strip of numbers.
  • Notices that never steal focus. A card in the corner for the plays you pick. Sound is off unless you want it.
  • Every game today. A scoreboard drawer, postseason series labels, and a quiet tab when another game gets close late.
  • Replays without spoilers. Games from 2023 on, at a pace that keeps the rhythm of each at-bat. Scores stay hidden while you pick.
  • Your look. Flat or 2.5D, solid or see-through. Every style is a file you can write yourself.

Want it on your phone?

Basesmall runs on computers for now. If enough people want a phone version, it gets a serious look. Leave your email and you'll get one message when there is news, either way.

Your phone

Kept: your email, the phone you picked, the language and the date, stored on Cloudflare. Used only for that one message, after which the whole list is deleted; never shared, nothing else sent. You can remove yourself any time: enter your email above and choose Remove my email.

Runs on your own computer.

The app needs no account and has no server, no ads, no analytics. Game data comes straight from MLB's public Stats API to your machine, and the app asks GitHub once at start-up whether a newer version exists (you can switch that off); an update is only downloaded when you press Update.
